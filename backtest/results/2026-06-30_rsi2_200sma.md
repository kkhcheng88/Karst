# Result — RSI-2 ± 200SMA filter on SPY / QQQ / SPMO

**Date:** 2026-06-30  **Script:** `backtest/experiments/exp_rsi2_filter.py`  **Tag:** ✅ verified (our own run)

## Question
Resolve the Layer-2 core open item: distillation §11.5 (RSI-2 + 200SMA filter
+258%, BEAT B&H) vs §11.6 (filter −84%). Does the 200SMA trend filter help RSI-2
on the 3 core ETFs?

## Method
- Connors RSI-2, long/flat. Entry `RSI(2) < 5` (+ `price > SMA200` when filter ON).
  Exit `RSI(2) > 70`.
- Look-ahead-safe: position on bar i decided from signals at i−1 (next-bar entry).
- Cost 1 bps/side. Raw close (no dividends). Cash earns 0 while flat.
- Wilder RSI. Data: defeatbeta (SPY/QQQ) + yfinance (SPMO). Full available history.

## Conclusions
1. **200SMA filter is NOT robust.** Marginal + on SPY, negative on QQQ/SPMO, flips
   by segment. Confirms §11.6 over §11.5.
2. **RSI-2 does not reliably beat B&H on return** (SPY/SPMO lose, QQQ ~ties) — only
   ~12% exposure. **But it slashes drawdown** (SPY −57%→−17%, QQQ −83%→−22%).
   → underlying TA timing = **drawdown control, not alpha**. Confirms §15 / §25.1.
3. **We could not reproduce §11.5's "+258% beats B&H"** (SPY 2016–2026: filter-ON
   Sharpe 0.48 < B&H 0.77). The distillation's headline TA edge is fragile to
   implementation and regime. → don't trust second-hand backtests.

## Caveats
Raw price (B&H understated by ~div yield); cash@0 understates RSI-2 risk-adjusted
return; SPMO history short + bull-skewed; fixed params (no optimization — so no
overfitting, but also unoptimized). DSR here is vs the 6 variants tested.

## Implication for Karst
The underlying-timing edge on indices is weak. **Real edge = the option structure
(VRP / premium selling), not TA timing of the underlying.** Keep RSI-2/200SMA as an
OPTIONAL drawdown overlay only; do not treat it as a return edge.

## Addendum (2026-06-30): exit-threshold sweep — `exp_rsi2_exit.py`
Swept exit at RSI(2) >70 / >80 / >90 / >95, filter on/off, 3 assets (24 variants, DSR-corrected).
- **Raising the exit makes it WORSE risk-adjusted.** Best Sharpe is at **>70 for all 3
  assets** (SPY 0.53/0.60, QQQ 0.70, SPMO 0.72). Higher exits raise exposure (12%→40%)
  → reintroduce drawdown (SPY DD −17%→−43%; QQQ −22%→−59%) without reliable CAGR gain;
  DSR degrades (QQQ >90 off → 66%).
- **Robustness implication:** the "RSI-2 = drawdown control, not alpha" conclusion is
  NOT an artifact of exit=70 — >70 is its *best case*.

## Addendum 2 (2026-06-30) — alpha / DD-distribution / win-stats — `exp_rsi2_alpha.py`
**This REVISES the "not alpha" claim above.** Measuring CAGR alone was the error —
RSI-2 is only ~12% invested, so low CAGR = low beta, not low skill. Jensen alpha
(removing beta) tells a different story. Entry {5,10} × exit {70,80,90,95}, filter OFF.

- **Significant positive alpha on SPY/QQQ at fast-exit/low-entry:** QQQ `<5 >70`
  alpha **6.53%/yr (t 2.9)**; SPY `<10 >80` **4.26% (t 2.4)**; SPY `<10 >70` 3.74%
  (t 2.1); QQQ `<10 >70` 5.15% (t 2.0). Real mean-reversion alpha, not diluted beta.
- **Alpha lives in the quick snap; holding longer destroys it.** exit 70→95 collapses
  the t-stat to ~0 (QQQ <5: 2.9→1.8→0.0→0.1). The fast >70 exit is harvesting the edge,
  not "cutting winners".
- **AvgDD tiny vs MaxDD:** at the good settings AvgDD ≈ −2% to −4% (MaxDD −17% to −34%)
  — usually barely underwater, very holdable. (User's point: don't judge on MaxDD alone.)
- **Win% ≈ 76% everywhere** — vanity metric, not the differentiator (§20.7).
- **entry <10** roughly doubles trades and raises SPY alpha/significance.

**Revised conclusion:** RSI-2 has a real but MODEST, low-capacity entry alpha on SPY/QQQ
(best at entry <5–10, exit >70–80). It STACKS with the option-structure (VRP) edge —
use RSI-2 to time CSP sells / LEAP entries on oversold dips. Caveats: in-sample
selection across 24 variants (DSR-corrected but not immune), small absolute alpha,
SPMO insignificant (short history), cash earns 0 (understates risk-adjusted return).
