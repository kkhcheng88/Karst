# Alpha360-style ML — breadth reveals a weak-but-real OOS signal (I was too quick to close the door)

2026-07-01. `exp_alpha360_ml.py` (11 sectors) + `exp_alpha360_broad.py` (110 stocks). Alpha360 is
raw normalized last-N OHLCV fed to an ML model (not 360 clever factors), so the right test is: train
a GBDT on it and measure OUT-OF-SAMPLE cross-sectional forward IC (strict walk-forward). The user
pushed back that 11 sectors is too narrow for ML and that we should explore broader scopes -- correct.

## Result
| universe | OOS forward IC | OOS top-N portfolio (net) |
|---|---|---|
| 11 sectors (narrow) | −0.010 (t −0.8, nothing) | top-3: 7.1%, loses to SPY |
| **110 stocks (broad)** | **+0.017 (t 3.5, significant)** | top-10: **26.6%**, Sharpe 0.89 |

Breadth turned a null (−0.01 on 9 sectors) into a statistically significant OOS signal (+0.017,
t 3.5 over 1444 weeks). Nonlinear ML on raw price/volume DOES rank a broad stock cross-section a
little. My earlier "price/volume door is closed" was confirmation bias — retracted.

## Honest temper — survivorship inflates the headline
- Universe = TODAY's SPDR top-10 holdings, backtested to 1993 → picking among known winners.
- Evidence: universe-EW alone returns 18% (vs SPY 10.7%) — that 7pp is the survivorship premium.
- So the fair excess is ML 26.6% vs EW 18% ≈ **+8.6pp**, driven by IC 0.017 (stock-level dispersion
  + breadth amplify a small IC).
- Survivorship can inflate the IC itself (the model learns the eventual winners' momentum). The
  definitive test needs a POINT-IN-TIME (non-selected) universe.

## Synthesis
- There IS a weak but real OOS price/volume ranking signal (IC ~0.017, significant), BELOW the 0.05
  "transformative" bar but not zero. Pure price/volume ML gets ~1/3 of the way to the bar.
- This reopens the quant thread and connects to the skill-curve framing: to push IC 0.017 → 0.05,
  either ADD new information (fundamentals/news = Phase 3, folded into the ML features) or improve
  the model. Quant and qualitative converge.
