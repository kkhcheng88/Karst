# Result — Core v2 base holding: SPY vs QQQ vs SPMO vs 50/50 SPY+QQQ (fixed LEAP sleeve + fixed benchmark, real data)

**Date:** 2026-07-07  **Script:** `backtest/experiments/exp_base_mix.py`  **Status:** active

## Question

Core v2 (`docs/2026-07-06_core_strategy_v2.md`) never tested WHY the base holding is SPY rather than QQQ or SPMO. This script A/Bs the base holding {SPY, QQQ, SPMO, 50/50 SPY+QQQ} while holding the LEAP sleeve (SPY+QQQ 50/50, pure 200SMA gate, b=15% NAV, C-monthly top-up, Δ=0.50 — the settled winner cell from `exp_core_topup.py`) and cash (15% NAV, ^IRX) FIXED. Benchmark is ALSO fixed at SPY B&H total return (HK 30% dividend withholding netted) — it is never re-pointed at the candidate base holding, because the question IS "beta bet vs alpha," and moving the yardstick would erase the answer.

## Method

- LEAP unit engine (`simulate_unit_path`) IMPORTED verbatim from `exp_leap_real_sweep.py`; portfolio top-up simulator (`simulate_portfolio_topup`, rule=C-monthly) IMPORTED verbatim from `exp_core_topup.py` — not reimplemented. Only the base-holding return series (axis below) is new plumbing in this script.
- Base-holding candidates use their OWN adjusted-close total return, HK 30% dividend withholding netted (same `r_net` formula as `exp_leap_real_sweep.build_underlying`, computed standalone here since the base holding needs no options engine / 200SMA / RSI-2 warm-up). 50/50 SPY+QQQ = daily-rebalanced average of each leg's own r_net (standard continuously-rebalanced-blend convention; cross-term error negligible at daily frequency).
- Windows: **Window A (PRIMARY)** = 2015-10-12+ (SPMO inception via `data.load(symbol, adjusted=True)`), all 4 base-holding cells share it. **Window B** = 2001+ (native start of the SPY+QQQ LEAP-sleeve common window, ^VXN availability — identical convention to `exp_core_topup.py`), 3 cells (SPMO excluded — no data pre-2015-10).
- Every cell reports BOTH a base-IV (m=0.85) row and an IV-damped (damp=0.4) row — same disclosure convention as `exp_core_topup.py` — affecting ONLY the (fixed) LEAP sleeve's option pricing, never the base-holding return series or the price-driven 200SMA gate.
- **Reading discipline (pre-registered, mechanical — not picked after seeing numbers)**: for every non-SPY cell, compare beta and alpha-t to the SPY cell in the SAME window x IV row. Beta higher by >=0.05 AND alpha-t does NOT rise by >=0.3 (and alpha level by >=0.5pp) -> **BETA BET** (a higher risk-appetite wager on the same market factor, not a new source of skill). Both rise meaningfully -> **ALPHA GAIN** (reported straight). Neither -> **similar/inconclusive**.
- Costs: 0.5%/side of option premium (LEAP legs only); base-holding trades (monthly top-up, annual/roll transfers) zero-cost (ETF shares). Sharpe = raw daily returns. CAPITAL = $500,000.

## Conclusions

**Window A (primary, 2015-10-12+, all 4 candidates on the identical window)**: ranked by
damp-IV Sharpe (the conservative reading) — SPMO (1.11) > 50/50 (1.05) ≈ QQQ (1.06) > SPY
(1.03), i.e. status-quo SPY has the LOWEST Sharpe of the four on this one short window.
But the mechanical beta/alpha split explains why for QQQ and 50/50: both raise portfolio
beta (QQQ 1.29/1.46 base/damp, 50/50 1.23/1.41) well above SPY-core's own 1.18/1.35, while
their alpha-t barely moves (QQQ t+3.7/+2.2 vs SPY's t+3.8/+2.0; 50/50 t+3.8/+2.2) — both
flagged **BETA BET** in both IV rows. SPMO is the one candidate that does NOT fit that
pattern: its beta (1.15 base / 1.32 damp) is FLAT-TO-LOWER than SPY-core's own beta
(1.18/1.35) while its alpha-t is flat-to-higher (t+3.9 vs +3.8 base; t+2.4 vs +2.0 damp) —
flagged **ALPHA GAIN** under the conservative damp model (inconclusive under base IV, since
neither the beta-drop nor the alpha-rise clears the pre-registered threshold there).

**Window B (2001+, long-history, no SPMO — the window that should govern a 30-year-horizon
decision)**: confirms the beta-bet reading for QQQ. Its beta (1.14/1.25 base/damp) is
genuinely higher than SPY-core's (1.07/1.18), while its alpha-t actually FALLS relative to
SPY-core (t+4.5 vs +5.4 base; t+2.7 vs +3.1 damp) despite a modestly higher raw CAGR (23.7%
vs 22.3% base) and materially worse MaxDD (-61.6% vs -54.5%). This is the textbook signature
of a beta bet: more return, more risk, LESS statistically confident alpha — flagged
**BETA BET** both IV rows. 50/50 SPY+QQQ sits almost exactly on top of SPY-core long-run
(β 1.11 vs 1.07, alpha-t +5.1 vs +5.4 base) — a wash, flagged **similar/inconclusive** both
rows, not an improvement.

**SPMO verdict**: the ONLY candidate with a genuine (mechanically-flagged) alpha-gain
signature — but it is visible on ONE window only, which is ALSO its entire 9.7-year,
single-regime history (2015-10-12+: a momentum-favorable bull market with one short 2022
drawdown and one 2020 V-shape). That is not enough evidence to swap a 30-year-horizon core's
base holding, per the pre-registered caveat — it needs either SPMO surviving a full
bear-market regime, or a longer-history momentum-factor proxy tested on the same harness,
before it can move from "intriguing" to "actionable."

**Bottom line**: on the long-history window that should decide this, QQQ and 50/50 SPY+QQQ
are risk-appetite choices (more beta, same-or-worse risk-adjusted alpha), not alpha
discoveries — swapping the base holding to either would be re-labeling a leverage decision as
skill. SPMO shows a promising but single-regime signal that is flagged, not acted on.

## Caveats

- **SPMO (Window A only)**: 9.7 years of history (2015-10-12 to date) = effectively ONE market regime (a momentum bull market with one short 2022 drawdown, one 2020 V-shape, one 2025 chop) — a 30-year-class conclusion cannot be drawn from Window A alone, however the numbers look. SPMO's own options market is thin/short-history (irrelevant here — SPMO never enters the LEAP sleeve — but relevant to any future idea of running the sleeve itself on SPMO). Expense ratio 0.13% (SPMO) vs 0.09% (SPY) is already netted into each fund's own adjusted-close total return; no separate adjustment applied.
- **QQQ / Window B dot-com coverage**: ^VXN availability starts 2001-01-23, AFTER QQQ's March-2000 peak, so Window B's QQQ B&H MaxDD (-70.4%) captures only the POST-peak continuation of the dot-com crash, not the full peak-to-trough figure (widely cited near -83%). Do not read this as QQQ's full crash-risk envelope.
- 50/50 SPY+QQQ base holding uses a daily-average-of-returns approximation for a continuously-rebalanced blend (not a literal daily-rebalance simulation with its own transaction costs) — immaterial at this frequency but noted for completeness.
- Single historical path, no bootstrap; multiple-testing correction not run here (only 4/3-cell grids, and the SPY cells are sanity anchors, not new trials competing with the already Bonferroni-corrected `exp_core_topup.py` winner).

## Implication

**Keep SPY as the core v2 base holding.** The long-history test (Window B, 2001+) is the one
that should govern a 30-year-horizon decision, and it shows QQQ and 50/50 SPY+QQQ do NOT add
real risk-adjusted alpha over SPY-core — QQQ adds beta (higher CAGR, worse MaxDD, LOWER
alpha-t), 50/50 adds roughly nothing (statistically indistinguishable from SPY-core). Neither
clears the pre-registered "ALPHA GAIN" bar on the window that matters.

SPMO's Window-A-only "alpha gain" flag (best Sharpe of the four, beta flat-to-lower than
SPY-core, alpha-t up under the conservative damp model) is worth tracking but not actionable
today: 9.7 years, one regime, thin evidence per the pre-registered caveat. Two concrete
triggers to revisit: (a) SPMO accumulating a full bear-market track record, or (b)
re-running this same harness (`build_core_only()` is a 6-line, symbol-agnostic function) on
a longer-history momentum-factor proxy (e.g. MTUM or a constructed momentum index) to see if
the Window-A signal survives a multi-regime test before it is trusted over SPY.

No change to `docs/2026-07-06_core_strategy_v2.md`'s base-holding choice (SPY) is warranted
by this result.
